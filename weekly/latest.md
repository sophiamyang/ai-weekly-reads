# An 86-minute guide to finding PMF | Rob Snyder (Author, The Power of Pull)

- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Podcast:** [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/an-86-minute-guide-to-finding-pmf)

## One-Sentence Takeaway
Product-market fit is discovered, not created: it exists when a specific person has an unavoidable project on their to-do list that they cannot complete with existing options, making it *weird not to buy* your solution.

## Short Summary
The PULL framework (Project, Unavoidable, List of options, Limitations) reframes product-market fit as a pre-existing condition in a customer’s workflow, not a feature or pain point to be sold. The strongest startup ideas emerge from firsthand experience—either the founder’s own unmet need or deep immersion in a customer’s world—rather than brainstorming or design partnerships.

The framework forces founders to identify *who* (a specific person with urgency) and *why now* (blocked by current tools/methods), then validate demand by testing whether prospects try to "rip the product out of your hands." Cold outreach works best when it’s intriguing (e.g., "Snoop Dogg invested in us") rather than pitchy, and early sales calls should focus on surfacing PULL, not demoing.

## Main Ideas
- **PULL is a customer state, not a product trait**: A person has *Project* (a job-to-be-done on their to-do list), *Unavoidable* (must be done now), *List of options* (existing tools/methods they’ve tried), and *Limitations* (why those options fail). All four must coexist for true pull.
- **Firsthand experience beats brainstorming**: The best ideas come from either solving your own urgent problem (and being honest about whether you’d pay for it) or embedding with customers to observe their stuck moments directly. Design partners rarely convert to real buyers.
- **Sales calls are discovery, not persuasion**: The goal is to surface PULL by asking, *"Why did you take this call?"* or *"What’s on your to-do list you’d pay someone to do now?"* (the "Hail Mary" question). If they don’t try to buy, iterate or pivot.
- **Pricing anchors to PULL**: Use the value of completing the project or the cost of current alternatives (the "L" in PULL) to set bounds. Example: If a pharma exec pays $50K/year for slow market research, charge $50K for a faster solution.
- **Avoid "push" signals**: If you’re convincing, explaining, or discounting, you’re likely missing PULL. Real pull feels like the customer is pulling *you* forward.

## Questions And Answers
**Q: How do you know if a sales call went well?**
A: The prospect leans in and asks, *"How do we get started?"* or *"Does this exist yet?"*—signs they’re trying to pull the solution from you. Anything less (e.g., "This is interesting for Q4") is not pull.

**Q: Should you offer design partnerships to early customers?**
A: No. Design partners often give feedback but rarely buy. People with real PULL want the problem solved *now*, not to co-design a product.

**Q: How much better does your product need to be than alternatives?**
A: It’s not about 10x improvement; it’s about unblocking a critical project. If existing options fail to get the job done, your solution is "infinitely better" to the stuck customer.

**Q: What’s the fastest way to validate an idea?**
A: Test a PULL hypothesis with two slides: (1) *"Here’s what I hear: You’re trying to do X now because Y, but your options [A, B] fail because Z. Right?"* and (2) *"Here’s how we might help. Does this fit?"* If they say *"Yes, how?"*—you’ve found pull.

## Notable Details
- Jump (AI note-taker for financial advisors) found PULL when advisors realized generic AI tools didn’t integrate with their CRMs or comply with SEC regulations—making it *unavoidable* to adopt Jump’s solution.
- A startup pivoting from applicant tracking to job descriptions closed its first deal after a Hail Mary question: *"Is there anything on your to-do list you’d pay us to do now?"*
- Outreach benchmarks: Aim for 30–40% LinkedIn connection accept rates, 30–50% reply rates, and 2–3 customer conversations/day. If below, refine your "interesting" hook.
- Early-stage deals can be closed with a mock-up or service-shaped offering (e.g., "AI product manager" delivered by humans) while validating PULL.

## Actionable Takeaways
- **Write a PULL hypothesis** for your idea: *"[Person] is trying to do [Project] now because [Unavoidable reason], but [List of options] fail because [Limitations]."*
- **Run the Hail Mary test**: In every customer call, ask, *"Is there anything on your to-do list you’d pay me to do right now?"*
- **Price to the "L"**: Anchor pricing to the cost of current alternatives or the value of completing the project.
- **Avoid design partners**: Focus on customers who are *already trying* to solve the problem, not those who’ll "help" you build it.
- **Watch for "weird not to buy"**: If prospects don’t react with urgency, pivot or iterate—don’t push.

# Democratizing Security for the Agentic Web with Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht

- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Podcast:** [No Priors](https://traffic.megaphone.fm/PDP2314551616.mp3)
- **Speaker:** Dane Knecht, CTO, Cloudflare

## One-Sentence Takeaway
The agentic web demands a security-first shift, with organizations prioritizing zero-trust architectures, AI-driven vulnerability discovery, and new economic models for agent-human coexistence.

## Short Summary
The transition to an agentic web has exposed critical security gaps, as legacy software and human-designed systems struggle under AI-driven attacks. Dane Knecht argues that security must become the primary focus for engineering teams over the next 18 months, with AI both exacerbating vulnerabilities (e.g., weekly Log4j-scale exploits) and enabling faster remediation through automated red-teaming and patching.

Zero-trust architectures outperform traditional castle-and-moat models, but the broader challenge remains: patching 70 years of software while redesigning development lifecycles for AI-native workflows. Cloudflare is addressing this with internal tools like Cloudflare OS, monetization gateways for agent-driven traffic, and collaborations like OpenAI’s Signals project to reduce crawling costs.

## Main Ideas
- **Security as the immediate priority**: Over the next 18 months, most engineering time will shift to security—both offensive (red-teaming) and defensive (patching, hardening)—as AI-driven vulnerability discovery outpaces human capacity. Cloudflare reports weekly incidents comparable to Log4j, with attackers ranging from nation-states to "agent kitties" (high school students using AI tools).
- **Zero-trust outperforms legacy models**: Organizations that adopted zero-trust security (device/flow-level controls) are better positioned than those relying on perimeter-based defenses, which are ill-suited for distributed, agentic systems.
- **Patching 70 years of software**: The backlog of vulnerabilities in existing codebases is vast, requiring a dual approach: automated discovery (via AI) and process changes (e.g., formal verification, safer languages) to reduce attack surfaces in new development.
- **Agent-human "messy middle"**: Current web infrastructure forces agents and humans to share the same rails, creating inefficiencies (e.g., agents parsing human-oriented pages). Long-term solutions may include parallel webs, agent-specific APIs, or micropayment systems to compensate content creators for agent-driven traffic.
- **Token spend will favor security**: Cloudflare predicts that in the near term, token budgets will prioritize security (e.g., red-teaming, code review) over feature development, with productivity gains expected once the backlog of critical vulnerabilities is addressed.

## Questions And Answers
- **Q: How will organizations balance security with feature development?**
  A: Security cannot come at the expense of shipping. Cloudflare plans to scale red-teaming (e.g., 4–5 dedicated teams) while automating patching processes, aiming to improve both security and velocity with AI assistance.

- **Q: What’s Cloudflare’s stance on open vs. closed models for cybersecurity?**
  A: Open models are inevitable, as bad actors will eventually access them. Restricting open models only disarms defenders who lack resources to develop proprietary tools.

- **Q: How can smaller organizations protect themselves?**
  A: Cloudflare advocates for democratized security via its platform, offering free protections (e.g., WAF rules for major vulnerabilities) to all users, not just paying customers.

## Notable Details
- Cloudflare reports a surge in vulnerabilities comparable to Log4j occurring **weekly**, up from ~1/year historically.
- A critical TLS/WebPKI key was secured with the seed value **"4"**, making it trivially reversible—highlighting how AI can exploit poor cryptographic practices.
- Cloudflare’s **Signals project with OpenAI** reduces agent crawling costs by proactively notifying AI labs of new content, benefiting both publishers and model providers.
- **Cloudflare OS** is an internal platform for secure AI usage, embedding company principles (e.g., "Codex" best practices) into agent workflows. It’s used more by go-to-market teams than engineers, accelerating tasks like email campaigns.
- Cloudflare hired **1,000 interns** in a year to drive AI-native workflows, observing that interns (and their managers) adopt AI tools faster than tenured employees.
- The company restructured to **AI-first workflows**, e.g., replacing a 3-team email process with a single AI tool that drafts, rewrites in brand voice, and routes for approval.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Audit legacy codebases for low-hanging vulnerabilities (e.g., poor cryptographic seeds, verbose AI-generated code) and prioritize attack surface reduction.
- Adopt zero-trust architectures if still relying on perimeter-based security, as agentic systems require granular, flow-level controls.
- Experiment with AI-driven red-teaming to stress-test systems, but pair it with human oversight to avoid false positives/negatives.
- Explore agent-specific APIs or monetization gateways (e.g., micropayments) to separate human and agent traffic, reducing inefficiencies and costs.
- Embed security and company principles into AI workflows early (e.g., via internal "OS" platforms) to scale governance without sacrificing speed.

# Why AI Compute Comes in Rectangles — Erik Bernhardsson, Modal CEO

- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Podcast:** [Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/modal-d)
- **Speaker:** Erik Bernhardsson, CEO and co-founder, Modal

## One-Sentence Takeaway
AI compute infrastructure must be rethought from the ground up for GPU workloads, and Modal’s success shows that developer experience, capacity aggregation, and multi-product building blocks (not just raw GPUs) are the keys to unlocking the next wave of AI applications.

***

## Short Summary

Modal provides a compute and storage platform optimized for AI workloads, particularly inference, sandboxes, and increasingly training and batch jobs. The core insight is that traditional cloud tools (Kubernetes, Docker) were not designed for expensive, bursty GPU workloads, so Modal rebuilt primitives like autoscaling, snapshotting, and capacity management from scratch. The company’s growth—50x in revenue and usage since 2021—was driven by early adoption of Stable Diffusion, then diversified across customers like Suno, Runway, and Cognition, proving that aggregation of demand and global capacity pooling can smooth utilization and improve efficiency.

The conversation also highlights the shifting economics of GPU supply: Modal now works with ~25 clouds, signs 3+ year deals, and debates whether to buy hardware or continue renting. Long-term, the company aims to be a multi-product platform (functions, sandboxes, training, batch) offering composable building blocks for AI applications, with a focus on developer (and agent) experience.

***

## Main Ideas

- **AI workloads require new infrastructure primitives**: Traditional cloud tools (e.g., Kubernetes) are optimized for steady, CPU-based workloads, not expensive, bursty GPU tasks. Modal rebuilt autoscaling, snapshotting, and capacity management to handle large model loading, high utilization, and rapid scaling, which existing tools couldn’t efficiently support.

- **Capacity aggregation and global pooling are competitive advantages**: Modal treats its capacity as a single global pool across regions and clouds, enabling efficient allocation for bursty or cyclical workloads. This aggregation smooths demand and allows the platform to serve thousands of customers without over-provisioning.

- **The GPU supply market is evolving rapidly**: Modal initially relied on spot and short-term reservations but now signs 3+ year deals with ~25 clouds (including hyperscalers and neoclouds) to secure capacity. The company debates buying hardware but prefers renting for capital efficiency, though it acknowledges the tradeoffs in operational complexity and financial risk.

- **Multi-product platforms will dominate AI infrastructure**: Modal’s revenue splits roughly into two-thirds (functions for inference) and one-third (sandboxes for code execution), with plans to expand into training, batch, and low-latency workloads. The goal is to provide composable building blocks (compute, storage, orchestration) for AI applications, not just raw GPUs.

- **Sandboxes are becoming a critical layer for AI agents**: Modal Sandboxes, initially slow to gain traction, exploded in 2024 as LLMs improved at generating full applications. Use cases include vibe coding (e.g., Lovable), background agents (e.g., Ramp), and reinforcement learning. Future differentiation will come from primitives like snapshotting, forking, and live migration to enable ephemeral, parallelized workloads.

***
***
## Questions And Answers

**Why rebuild infrastructure for AI instead of using Kubernetes?**
Existing tools were designed for steady, CPU-centric workloads with low utilization costs. AI workloads demand high GPU utilization, fast scaling, and efficient loading of large models—requirements that Kubernetes and Docker weren’t optimized for. Modal built custom autoscalers, schedulers, and snapshotting to handle these constraints.

**How does Modal compete with neoclouds and hyperscalers for GPU capacity?**
Modal aggregates capacity from ~25 clouds, including smaller or riskier providers offering niche hardware (e.g., A100s in Armenia). It signs long-term deals and pools capacity globally, while neoclouds often pre-sell capacity to large buyers (e.g., Anthropic) to secure financing. Modal’s advantage lies in its ability to serve diverse, bursty workloads efficiently.

**What’s the long-term outlook for GPU prices and supply?**
Hardware prices tend to decline over time, but the current scarcity (driven by NVIDIA’s dominance and high demand) has reversed this trend. Bernhardsson expects prices to eventually drop but warns that buying at the peak could leave providers with overpriced assets. Modal is "long GPU prices" due to its locked-in reservations but is cautious about overcommitting.

**How are sandboxes evolving for AI agents?**
Sandboxes are shifting from "lift-and-shift" dev environments to ephemeral, parallelized execution units for agents. Modal is investing in snapshotting, forking, and live migration to reduce overhead (e.g., spinning up a sandbox in 100ms) and enable massive parallelism (e.g., running each test in a CI suite in its own sandbox).

***
***
## Notable Details

- Modal’s revenue grew **50x since 2021**, driven by early adoption of Stable Diffusion and later diversification across customers like Suno, Runway, and Cognition.
- **Modal Sandboxes** went from negligible revenue in mid-2023 to **~1/3 of total revenue** in 2025, fueled by vibe coding (e.g., Lovable), background agents (e.g., Ramp), and reinforcement learning.
- Modal now works with **~25 clouds**, signing **3+ year deals** to secure capacity, and has a **10-person team** dedicated to managing bare-metal hardware and procurement.
- The company’s **global capacity pool** treats all regions as a single resource, enabling efficient allocation for bursty workloads.
- **Sandbox snapshotting** is a key focus to enable ephemeral, parallelized workloads (e.g., running each test in a CI suite in its own sandbox).
- Modal uses **AI agents internally** to probe for security vulnerabilities, treating it as an "escape room" challenge to harden its infrastructure.

***
***
## Actionable Takeaways

- **Watch for infrastructure players that aggregate demand**: Modal’s success shows that pooling capacity globally and serving diverse workloads can outperform niche neoclouds focused solely on raw GPU sales.
- **Evaluate multi-product platforms over single-purpose tools**: Modal’s shift from functions to sandboxes, training, and batch suggests that composable building blocks will be more valuable than standalone products.
- **Monitor GPU supply dynamics**: Long-term deals and alternative accelerators (AMD, TPUs) may reshape the market, but NVIDIA’s dominance and TSMC/ASML bottlenecks remain key constraints.
- **Prioritize ephemeral compute for agents**: Invest in primitives like snapshotting and forking to enable parallelized, short-lived workloads for AI agents.
- **Use AI for defensive security**: Deploy agents to probe for vulnerabilities internally, but don’t assume they replace human expertise or traditional cybersecurity vendors.

# The Recommendation Engine for Commodity Traders — Anekha Sokhal, Moshi

- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Podcast:** [Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/moshi)
- **Speaker:** Anekha Sokhal, CEO of Moshi

## One-Sentence Takeaway
Moshi builds a real-time, AI-driven recommendation engine for commodity traders that surfaces second- and third-order market signals from high-volume structured and unstructured data, addressing the workflow gap between raw data and actionable insights.

## Short Summary
Moshi processes vast streams of market, fundamental, and alternative data (e.g., news, social media, proprietary feeds) to deliver personalized, confidence-scored insights for traders. The system combines technical indicators, supply-demand time series, and unstructured sources into a connected graph, then pushes relevant updates to users—akin to a Netflix-style recommendation system for financial markets.

The conversation highlights the tension between data abundance and user adoption: even sophisticated dashboards fail if they don’t integrate seamlessly into existing workflows. Moshi’s approach emphasizes push-based UI, proprietary models for sensitive data, and filtering noise (e.g., divergent news sources) to surface actionable divergence or consensus.

## Main Ideas
- **Data volume and variety**: Moshi ingests and correlates structured (futures, technical indicators), fundamental (supply-demand), and unstructured (news, X/Twitter, audio, PDFs) data at scale—processing ~300 new data points in the time it takes to speak a sentence.
- **Recommendation as a core primitive**: The system acts like a "Netflix for traders," surfacing second/third-order effects (e.g., an energy equities event impacting oil) via a connected graph, rather than relying on linear news streams or manual analysis.
- **UI as a first-class problem**: Effective AI tools must push insights proactively and fit into existing workflows; otherwise, even technically sound solutions (e.g., custom dashboards) go unused.
- **Proprietary vs. third-party models**: For sensitive or proprietary data (e.g., internal PDFs), Moshi avoids third-party vendors to control data handling and model customization, though this requires in-house engineering capacity.
- **Confidence scoring for noisy sources**: When presenting divergent signals (e.g., conflicting reports on LNG shipments), Moshi assigns confidence scores and highlights disagreements as potential opportunities.

## Questions And Answers
- **How do you prioritize noisy data like X/Twitter?**
  Moshi filters by trusted voices (e.g., industry suppliers) and flags divergences (e.g., Reuters vs. Bloomberg vs. WSJ) as actionable signals, since disagreements can indicate market opportunities.

- **Why build your own vision-language models for PDFs instead of using vendors?**
  To avoid sending proprietary data to third parties and to tailor models to domain-specific needs (e.g., commodity trading documents), though this is only feasible with an in-house engineering team.

- **What’s the biggest adoption hurdle for AI tools in trading?**
  Users often ignore dashboards or emails if the UI doesn’t integrate into their workflow; push-based, addictive (Netflix-like) designs increase engagement.

- **How do you handle the "build vs. buy" pressure on AI engineers?**
  Many engineers are tasked with single-handedly replacing teams of 10, leading to burnout; Moshi positions itself as a turnkey solution for such gaps.

## Notable Details
- Moshi processes ~300 new data points in ~4 seconds (the time to speak a short sentence).
- Alternative data examples: LNG ship tracking, satellite imagery, X/Twitter firehose (noted as historically costly, e.g., $300K/month for hedge funds).
- Bloomberg chat was used for option pricing via NLP parsing in Sokhal’s trading days.
- Moshi’s team includes engineers with NASA experience, enabling in-house model development.
- Post-conference inbound led to back-to-back meetings, prompting Moshi to organize webinars to scale engagement.

## Actionable Takeaways
- For trading desks: Evaluate tools that push insights (not just pull) and highlight divergences in trusted sources.
- For AI engineers: Prioritize workflow integration (e.g., push notifications, minimal clicks) over technical sophistication to drive adoption.
- For startups: Consider proprietary models for sensitive data, but weigh the cost of in-house engineering vs. third-party vendors.
- Watch for: The rise of "Netflix-style" recommendation systems in niche verticals (e.g., commodities) where second-order effects matter.

# The New Apex Predator: CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz on Adversaries Armed With AI Agents

- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Podcast:** [No Priors](https://traffic.megaphone.fm/PDP8573582914.mp3)
- **Speaker:** George Kurtz, President and CEO, CrowdStrike

## One-Sentence Takeaway
AI-equipped adversaries are flattening the threat pyramid by giving low-skill attackers nation-state-level capabilities, forcing defenders to adopt AI-driven security to keep pace.

## Short Summary
AI agents are enabling hacktivists and e-crime groups to execute attacks with the sophistication previously reserved for nation-states, accelerating both the discovery of vulnerabilities and the speed of exploitation—with breakout times now as low as 27 seconds. Defenders must leverage their unique advantages (e.g., system knowledge, architectural diagrams, code access) to deploy AI that can outmaneuver offensive AI, but current frontier models often lack the agility or guardrails for security-specific tasks.

CrowdStrike is addressing this by building specialized security models (e.g., Red Tempest, Blue Solano) trained on proprietary threat data, while also investing in agent visibility and control to combat "shadow AI" in enterprises.

## Main Ideas
- AI agents are collapsing the traditional adversary pyramid (hacktivists → e-crime → nation-states) by democratizing advanced tradecraft, enabling low-skill actors to execute high-sophistication attacks at unprecedented speed.
- Attackers now iterate in seconds or minutes (e.g., 27-second breakout times observed), using prompts—not just malware—to dynamically pivot, gather intelligence, and exploit systems, making detection harder.
- Defenders’ key advantages (system knowledge, code access, architectural context) must be embedded into AI-driven security to outpace offensive AI, but frontier models often refuse security-related tasks, forcing reliance on open-weight or specialized models.
- CrowdStrike argues that defense can achieve parity or superiority by combining frontier or custom models (e.g., Nemotron-based Blue Solano) with proprietary threat data, digital twins for simulation, and harnesses that improve model efficiency and cost (e.g., remediation at 3 cents vs. $10 for frontier models, according to the guest).
- Open-weight models are increasingly viable for attackers and defenders alike, with enterprises favoring them for cost and control, though they may lag frontier models by months—a gap many organizations find acceptable.

## Questions And Answers
**Q: How are AI agents changing the threat landscape?**
A: They’re flattening the adversary pyramid, giving hacktivists nation-state-level capabilities, and accelerating attacks to breakout times as low as 27 seconds by using dynamic prompts instead of static malware.

**Q: Why can’t defenders rely solely on frontier models for security?**
A: Frontier models often refuse security-related tasks (e.g., generating cybersecurity content), forcing defenders to use open-weight or specialized models like CrowdStrike’s Red Tempest and Blue Solano, which are trained on proprietary threat data.

**Q: What’s CrowdStrike’s approach to AI-driven defense?**
A: It combines custom models (post-trained on 15+ years of security data), digital twins for adversarial simulation, and a harness to improve model efficiency and reduce costs (e.g., 3-cent remediation vs. $10 for frontier models, according to the guest).

**Q: Are open-weight models sufficient for enterprise security?**
A: Many enterprises find them "good enough" for most tasks, reserving frontier models for high-stakes use cases, as open-weight models are cheaper and nearly as capable for routine operations.

## Notable Details
- Breakout time (time from initial access to lateral movement) has dropped to an average of 29 minutes, with some attacks executing in **27 seconds**, according to CrowdStrike’s observations.
- CrowdStrike’s **SafeMind** system includes **Red Tempest** (offensive model) and **Blue Solano** (defensive model), trained on proprietary data and fine-tuned for security tasks.
- CrowdStrike reports its security models can perform remediation tasks for **3 cents** compared to **$10** for frontier models, due to post-training and harness optimizations.
- **Shadow AI** (unmonitored agent deployments) is a major enterprise risk, with employees potentially running **90+ agents** without oversight, according to the guest.
- Open-weight models (e.g., GLM, Qin) are frequently used by attackers because they lack guardrails and can run on local hardware (e.g., two Spark DGXs or high-end Macs).
- CrowdStrike and NVIDIA collaborate on a **cyber superintelligence lab** to iterate red/blue models against digital twins of customer environments.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Audit for **shadow AI**: Identify and monitor all agents operating in your environment, as unchecked deployments create blind spots.
- Prioritize **defensive AI parity**: Ensure defenders have access to models and tools at least as capable as those used by attackers, including specialized security models.
